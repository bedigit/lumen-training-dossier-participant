Exercise 3 — A BRD in your company's standard, from an example

Tool: Word file creation, guided by an existing document. Deliverable: a Business Requirements Document for the LUMEN project as it stands, with the same structure as a BRD your company already wrote.

You don't start from a blank page, and you don't describe the format yourself: you give the AI a document that already follows the house standard. The example gives the form. It never gives the facts.

  1. Copy the example BRD exemple/BRD_exemple_PIM_anonymise.pdf to raw. It is the functional specification of another project (PIM, post-incident management for a rail operator), anonymised: people, company and systems are fictitious.
  2. Run the prompt. Stop at step 1 and check the standard it describes against the example.
  3. Confirm, and get the BRD.
  4. Open the example and your BRD side by side: same sections, same order, same numbering?
  5. Search your BRD for anything that belongs to the example's project.
Example: raw/BRD_exemple_PIM_anonymise.pdf. It is a BRD from another project.
Use it only as our company standard: structure, numbering, tone and level of detail.
Never reuse its content.

Subject of the new BRD: the LUMEN project as the wiki describes it today.
Sources: the wiki only. Read wiki/index.md first, then the pages on the scope, the business
needs, the requirements, the business rules, the use case catalogue and the target process.
Work in two steps.

Step 1: describe the standard you see in the example: the sections in order,
how requirements are numbered and prioritised, what goes in tables, the tone.
Then, for each section, say which source you will fill it from.
List the sections for which we have no source. Stop and show me this.

Step 2, once I confirm: write the BRD in wiki/deliverables/BRD-lumen.docx,
with the same sections, in the same order, with the same heading names.
If you cannot write a Word file, write Markdown with the same headings.
- Number and prioritise the functional requirements the way the example does.
- Cite the wiki page and the rule for each requirement.
- Say for each requirement whether it is delivered, analysed or still to analyse, as the wiki does.
- Use only what the wiki says, not what you know about invoicing tools in general.
- If no source covers a section, keep the heading and write "To be confirmed". Don't fill it in.
- Put what the wiki leaves open under the assumptions, as questions with an owner.
At the end, list where you departed from the example's structure, and why.

Check:

  • Same sections, in the same order, with the same heading names as the example?
  • Are the requirements numbered and prioritised the way the example does it?
  • Does anything come from the example's project: a system, a role, a dependency, a figure?
  • Which sections had no source? Are they marked "To be confirmed", or filled in?
  • Is a use case that is only analysed written as delivered? Check its status in the catalogue.
  • Does anything come from general knowledge rather than from the wiki?
  • Pick three requirements and find their wiki page and rule.
LUMEN is a fictional project, written for this training.
